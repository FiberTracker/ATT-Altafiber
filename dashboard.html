1. The Cincinnati Enclave — an incumbent-grade hole in AT&T's Midwest map

Hamilton (253K), Butler (100K), Clermont (68K) and Warren (53K) counties in Ohio plus Northern Kentucky form a contiguous six-county enclave where Cincinnati Bell — not AT&T — is the incumbent. AT&T wireline inside it: literally zero in Hamilton, Clermont and the core NKY counties; the only real AT&T pocket is the Middletown corner of Butler County (~11.0K copper BSLs, just 20 fiber) plus ~680 copper lines in Warren. This is the only major metro-scale gap in AT&T's OH/KY/IN region, already ~98% fiber-lit at ~48% ILEC penetration7 — finished infrastructure, not a build project. Note the surrounding rural ring (Ross/Chillicothe, Allen/Lima, Wayne-IN/Richmond, Preble, Darke) is largely not AT&T either — it belongs to Brightspeed, Verizon-Frontier, Horizon and independents — so the enclave borders AT&T's metros, not a continuous AT&T wall.

altafiber's ~$200M / 2,300-route-mile Dayton edge-out (70K addresses passed at midway, per Feb 2026 company release8) is being built inside AT&T ILEC territory. BDC already shows 31K locations where both operators have FTTP and another 21K where altafiber fiber sits on AT&T copper. Every quarter this build continues, returns on AT&T's own Dayton fiber deteriorate — and the target gets more expensive. Acquiring altafiber converts a funded competitor build into owned expansion.

altafiber has begun edging into metro Columbus — AT&T's largest Ohio fiber market (138K fiber BSLs in Franklin County alone): Dublin (~17K homes) and the Westerville public-private partnership (15K homes, announced Jul 20269). BDC shows 11.4K locations already live, two-thirds of them on AT&T copper or fiber. Note the balance of the announced expansion program points away from AT&T: Richmond IN (Verizon-Frontier territory), Eaton, Greenville, Wilmington OH sit in non-AT&T incumbent towns — only Washington Court House (~850 AT&T copper lines) and Carrollton KY (~2.0K AT&T copper) touch AT&T territory, and only Dayton/Columbus attack AT&T fiber. For an acquirer that means the growth pipeline is mostly non-cannibalistic white space, and the two live collision fronts simply stop being a problem — the 400K new-market program10 becomes a complement instead of a threat.

AT&T sells wireless throughout the Cincinnati DMA but has no owned wireline to bundle with it — the fiber+mobility convergence engine (42.5% of AT&T fiber households take AT&T postpaid; 45% ex-Lumen) has no fuel here. altafiber brings 518K strategic internet subscribers and a ~48%-penetrated ILEC core on day one — immediately monetizable through convergence lift and churn reduction on both products, the same logic AT&T used for the Lumen mass-markets deal.

1.44M finished FTTP passings is a meaningful step toward AT&T's 60M-by-2030 goal — equivalent to ~4 months of AT&T's entire national build engine, delivered penetrated instead of empty. And the shelf is nearly bare: 2026 has seen GoNetSpeed/Greenlight and i3 go to T-Mobile JVs, Ripple to Verizon-aligned Eaton, GFiber+Astound combine under Stonepeak, and Hotwire to Brookfield13. altafiber is the last incumbent-grade, fiber-complete metro enclave inside AT&T's own region. Hawaiian Telcom (467K passings, 27% pen., statewide fiber ~complete 2026-27) is separable for a buyer that wants only the Midwest — or a growth option in a state where AT&T has no wireline at all.

Methodology & Assumptions

  • Source: FCC BDC December 2025 filing (J26, published mid-2026). Six state-level CSVs: AT&T (provider 130077) and Cincinnati Bell Inc. d/b/a altafiber (provider 130254) in OH, KY, IN; plus Hawaii for altafiber (Hawaiian Telcom files under the same provider ID).
  • Technology scope: FTTP (code 50) + copper/DSL (code 10) for both operators. Copper marks legacy ILEC serving territory. AT&T Internet Air fixed wireless deliberately excluded — it would falsely mark all geography as AT&T-served.
  • Overlap: exact match on FCC location_id across both operators, decomposed by technology pair (FTTP×FTTP, altafiber-fiber×AT&T-copper, etc.).
  • Incremental: altafiber locations with no AT&T wireline record of any technology — net new serviceable footprint to AT&T.
  • BDC vs reported passings: BDC records 711K tri-state + 271K Hawaii BSLs vs 1.44M company-reported FTTP passings (1.47x). Standard gap: multi-dwelling units collapse into single BSLs, and "passings" counts serviceable addresses under looser rules. Both figures shown; BSL-level overlap ratios are unaffected.
  • Residential mix: altafiber files most locations under both residential and business codes, so a residential share is not derivable from BDC for this filer (not shown).
  • County/block-group geometry: Census TIGERweb 2020 (Layer 82 counties, Layer 8 block groups), fetched at view time.
